How the knowledge layer worksCONTEXT ONLY

AviPulsa keeps three evidence layers separate: Reference knowledge (published external evidence), Farm baseline (this farm's own normalized history), and Current observation (today's measurements). A reference can supplement a new farm, but it does not automatically create a disease alert.

Water — laying hens

Ontario reference: about 180–320 L/1,000 birds/day, with a typical value around 250 L/1,000 birds/day under average Ontario conditions. Temperature and egg production can materially affect water consumption.

Source: Ontario Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs, Water requirements of livestock. Reference is retained with its geography and conditions rather than treated as a universal threshold.

Daily monitoring signals

Ontario poultry biosecurity guidance recommends daily flock checks and monitoring mortality, feed/water consumption and egg production because changes can provide early indications that a flock needs attention. AviPulsa uses this evidence as contextual support, not as a diagnostic rule.

Source: Ontario Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs, Biosecurity recommendations for commercial poultry flocks in Ontario.

Evidence hierarchy

  1. Government / veterinary authority / recognized standards
  2. Peer-reviewed poultry research
  3. Commercial breeder and technical manuals
  4. Validated farm-derived evidence

Every future knowledge record should carry source, geography, bird type, age/stage, environmental conditions, unit, evidence type and verification date.

Important: Published reference ranges describe expected conditions under stated circumstances. They are not diagnoses and should not be used alone to label a flock unhealthy.
